Tax Compliance and Filing Guide for MSMEs

Overview:
MSME owners must comply with various tax requirements including income tax, GST, and other indirect taxes.

Income Tax for MSMEs:

1. Individual/Sole Proprietor
   - File ITR-3 form annually
   - Financial year: April 1 - March 31
   - Filing deadline: July 31
   - Exemption limit: ₹2.5 lakhs (general), ₹5 lakhs (senior citizens)
   - Tax rates: Progressive (5% to 30%)

2. Partnership Firm
   - File ITR-4 (if eligible under Section 44AD)
   - File ITR-5 for detailed assessment
   - Separate return from individual partners
   - Deadline: July 31

3. LLP and Company
   - File ITR-6 (if not claiming deductions)
   - File ITR-4 or ITR-5 (with deductions)
   - Audit required if turnover > ₹1 crore
   - Tax rate: Flat 25% (companies), 30% (individuals)

Tax Deductions Available:

- Interest on borrowed capital
- Depreciation on assets
- Rent and utilities
- Salaries and wages
- Professional fees
- Insurance premiums
- Travel and conveyance
- Meal and entertainment (up to 50%)
- Office supplies and materials
- Depreciation on equipment
- Bad debts

GST Compliance:

1. GST Registration
   - Mandatory if turnover > ₹40 lakhs/₹20 lakhs (services)
   - Online registration: gst.gov.in
   - Processing time: 3-7 days
   - Cost: Free

2. GST Returns
   - GSTR-1: Sales (monthly/quarterly)
   - GSTR-2A: Auto-populated from suppliers
   - GSTR-3B: Summary return (monthly/quarterly)
   - GSTR-9: Annual return
   - Deadlines: 11-20th of following month

3. Input Tax Credit (ITC)
   - Claim ITC on purchases
   - Match invoices with GSTR-2A
   - Maintain proper documentation
   - Can reduce tax liability significantly

Tax Planning Tips:

1. Keep detailed records
   - Maintain all bills and invoices
   - Keep bank statements and receipts
   - Document all business transactions
   - Use accounting software

2. Avail exemptions
   - Section 44AD: Presumptive income scheme (turnover < ₹1 crore, ≤ 6% tax)
   - Section 44ADA: For professionals
   - Section 44AB: For service providers

3. Time-based planning
   - File GST returns on time
   - File income tax return before July 31
   - Pay advance tax if required
   - Claim refunds promptly

4. Expense management
   - Segregate personal and business expenses
   - Claim all eligible deductions
   - Maintain supporting documents
   - Use professional accountant

Important Dates:

- Quarterly GST due: 11th of next month
- Monthly GST due: 20th of next month
- Income tax filing: July 31
- Advance tax: June 15, Sept 15, Dec 15, March 15
- TDS deposits: 7th of following month

Documentation Required:

- Pan Card and Aadhaar
- GST registration certificate
- Business address proof
- Bank account statements
- Invoices and bills
- Purchase orders and delivery notes
- Payment receipts
- Employees' records (if applicable)
- Asset purchase documents

Penalties for Non-Compliance:

- Late GST return: ₹100-500 per day
- No GST registration: ₹25,000 (first offense)
- Incorrect information: Up to ₹25,000
- Late income tax return: ₹5,000-10,000

How to File:

1. Maintain accounting records (Tally, QuickBooks, etc.)
2. Prepare financial statements
3. Calculate GST liability
4. File GSTR-1 (if registered)
5. File income tax return (ITR)
6. Pay any outstanding tax
7. Maintain copies of all returns

Professional Help:
- Consult CA for complex returns
- Use tax software for filing
- Join MSME association for guidance
- Contact MSME helpline: 1800-11-6446

Resources:

- GST Portal: gst.gov.in

ADVANCED TAX COMPLIANCE GUIDE:

Income Tax Filing - Detailed by Business Type:

1. Sole Proprietorship/Individual:

ITR Forms:
- ITR-1 (Sahaj): Salary + 1 house property + other sources up to ₹50 lakhs
- ITR-2: Multiple house properties, capital gains, foreign income
- ITR-3: Business/profession income
- ITR-4 (Sugam): Presumptive income (Section 44AD/44ADA/44AE)

Choosing Right Form:
Use ITR-3 if:
- Business turnover > ₹2 crores
- Maintaining books of accounts
- Want to claim actual expenses
- Multiple businesses

Use ITR-4 if:
- Business turnover ≤ ₹2 crores
- Opting for presumptive taxation
- Don't want detailed accounting
- Simple compliance

Presumptive Taxation Schemes:

Section 44AD (Business):
- Turnover up to ₹2 crores
- Deemed profit: 8% of turnover (or 6% if digital receipts)
- No need to maintain books
- Cannot claim depreciation separately
- Must file ITR-4
- Once opted, must continue for 5 years

Example:
Turnover: ₹50 lakhs
Digital receipts: ₹30 lakhs
Cash receipts: ₹20 lakhs
Taxable income: (₹30L × 6%) + (₹20L × 8%) = ₹1.8L + ₹1.6L = ₹3.4 lakhs

Section 44ADA (Professionals):
- Gross receipts up to ₹50 lakhs
- Deemed profit: 50% of receipts
- Applicable to: Doctors, lawyers, CAs, architects, etc.
- No audit required
- Cannot opt out for 5 years once chosen

Section 44AE (Goods Transport):
- Deemed profit per vehicle per month:
  * Heavy vehicle (>12 tons): ₹1,000/month
  * Other vehicles: ₹7,500/month
- Multiply by months owned
- Actual receipts not needed

Tax Rates (FY 2025-26):

Old Regime:
- Up to ₹2.5 lakhs: NIL
- ₹2.5-5 lakhs: 5%
- ₹5-10 lakhs: 20%
- Above ₹10 lakhs: 30%
Plus: 4% cess
Rebate: Up to ₹12,500 if income < ₹5 lakhs

New Regime (Default from FY 2023-24):
- Up to ₹3 lakhs: NIL
- ₹3-6 lakhs: 5%
- ₹6-9 lakhs: 10%
- ₹9-12 lakhs: 15%
- ₹12-15 lakhs: 20%
- Above ₹15 lakhs: 30%
Plus: 4% cess
Rebate: Full tax if income < ₹7 lakhs

Note: New regime doesn't allow most deductions (80C, 80D, HRA, etc.)

2. Partnership Firm:

ITR Forms:
- ITR-5: All partnership firms
- File separate from partners' individual returns

Tax Rate:
- Flat 30% + surcharge + cess
- No basic exemption limit
- Partners' salary/interest deductible before tax

Deductions Allowed:
✓ Partners' remuneration (as per section 40(b))
✓ Interest to partners (max 12% p.a.)
✓ Business expenses
✓ Depreciation
✗ No 80C, 80D, etc. deductions

Maximum Remuneration to Partners:
- On first ₹3 lakhs of book profit: ₹1.5 lakhs or 90% of book profit (whichever higher)
- On balance: 60%

3. LLP:

ITR Form: ITR-5
Tax Rate: 30% flat + surcharge + cess
Similar to partnership firm treatment

4. Private Limited Company:

ITR Form: ITR-6
Tax Rates:
- Turnover/gross receipts ≤ ₹400 crores: 25%
- Turnover/gross receipts > ₹400 crores: 30%
- New manufacturing companies (set up after 01-10-2019): 15% (optional, with conditions)

Plus surcharge:
- Income ₹1-10 crores: 7%
- Income > ₹10 crores: 12%
Plus cess: 4%

Advance Tax Payment:

Mandatory if tax liability > ₹10,000

Due Dates:
- 15th June: 15% of tax
- 15th September: 45% of tax
- 15th December: 75% of tax
- 15th March: 100% of tax

Interest for Non/Short Payment:
- Section 234B: 1% per month (for balance tax)
- Section 234C: 1% per month (for advance tax default)

TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) Compliance:

Major Sections for MSMEs:

Section 194C - Payments to Contractors:
Rate:
- Individual/HUF: 1% (transport: 0%)
- Others: 2%
Threshold: ₹30,000 per payment or ₹1,00,000 per year
Due Date: 7th of next month

Section 194J - Professional Fees:
Rate: 10% (2% for technical services)
Applicable to: CA, lawyer, doctor, architect, engineer fees
Threshold: ₹30,000 per year
Due Date: 7th of next month

Section 194I - Rent:
Rate:
- Plant & machinery: 2%
- Land & building: 10%
Threshold: ₹2,40,000 per year
Due Date: 30th of next month (property), 7th (equipment)

Section 194H - Commission:
Rate: 5%
Threshold: ₹15,000 per year
Due Date: 7th of next month

Section 194Q - Purchase of Goods:
Rate: 0.1%
Applicable: Buyers with turnover > ₹10 crores
Threshold: ₹50 lakhs of purchases from single seller
Due Date: 7th of next month

TDS Filing Returns:
- Form 24Q: Salary TDS (quarterly)
- Form 26Q: Non-salary TDS (quarterly)
- Form 27Q: TDS on foreign payments (quarterly)

Due Dates:
- Q1 (Apr-Jun): 31st July
- Q2 (Jul-Sep): 31st October
- Q3 (Oct-Dec): 31st January
- Q4 (Jan-Mar): 31st May

Late Filing Penalty:
- ₹200 per day (max: TDS amount)

Non-Deduction Penalty:
- 100% of TDS amount + interest

TCS (Tax Collected at Source):

Applicable Transactions:
- Sale of goods > ₹50 lakhs to single buyer (if seller's turnover > ₹10 crores)
- Sale of motor vehicles > ₹10 lakhs
- Overseas tour packages
- Sale of goods through e-commerce

Section 206C(1H) - Sale of Goods:
Rate: 0.1%
Threshold: ₹50 lakhs of sales to buyer
Seller's turnover: Must exceed ₹10 crores

TCS Return: Form 27EQ (quarterly)

GST Compliance Deep-Dive:

Input Tax Credit (ITC) Optimization:

Eligibility Conditions:
1. Tax invoice from registered supplier
2. Goods/services received
3. Supplier has paid tax to government
4. Return filed
5. Used for business purposes

ITC Matching Process:
1. Your purchases shown in supplier's GSTR-1
2. Auto-populated in your GSTR-2A/2B
3. Reconcile with your purchase register
4. Claim matched ITC in GSTR-3B
5. Annual reconciliation in GSTR-9

Common ITC Errors:
✗ Claiming ITC before receiving goods
✗ Not reconciling with GSTR-2A/2B
✗ Claiming on blocked items (vehicles, food)
✗ Claiming without supplier payment
✗ Time limit crossed (earlier of annual return or Nov of next FY)

ITC Reversal Required When:
- Goods/services for personal use
- Exempted supply
- Lost/stolen/destroyed goods
- Non-payment to supplier beyond 180 days

Blocked Credits:
- Motor vehicles (except for specific business use)
- Food and beverages (except outward taxable supply)
- Outdoor catering
- Membership of clubs
- Health and fitness
- Life and health insurance
- Travel benefits to employees (personal)
- Works contract for immovable property (personal use)

E-Way Bill Compliance:

When Required:
- Movement of goods value > ₹50,000
- Interstate: Mandatory
- Intrastate: State-specific (many states mandate)

Generation:
- Before movement of goods
- Can be generated by supplier, recipient, or transporter
- On www.ewaybillgst.gov.in

Details Required:
- Invoice/challan number and date
- Value of goods
- HSN code
- Place of delivery
- Transporter details
- Vehicle number

Validity Period:
Distance | Validity
< 200 km | 1 day
200-400 km | 2 days
400-600 km | 3 days
Each additional 200 km | +1 day

Extension: Can be extended if delay due to valid reasons

Penalties:
- No e-way bill: 100% of tax amount or ₹10,000 (whichever higher)
- Wrong details: Same penalty

GST Annual Return (GSTR-9):

Due Date: 31st December of next financial year
Applicable: Turnover > ₹2 crores

Sections:
- Part I: Basic details
- Part II: Outward supplies (from GSTR-1)
- Part III: ITC availed (from GSTR-3B)
- Part IV: Demands and refunds
- Part V: Tax paid
- Part VI: Refund claimed

Reconciliation:
- Match GSTR-1 total with GSTR-9
- Match GSTR-3B ITC with GSTR-9
- Reconcile with books of accounts
- Report any discrepancies

GST Audit (GSTR-9C):

Applicable: Turnover > ₹5 crores
Certified by: CA/CMA
Due Date: 31st December

Reconciliation Statements:
1. Turnover as per audited financials vs GSTR-9
2. Taxable turnover vs books
3. ITC claimed vs books
4. Tax paid vs books
5. Reasons for differences

Audit Requirements:

Tax Audit (Section 44AB):

Mandatory if:
- Business: Turnover > ₹1 crore (₹10 crores if cash receipts < 5%)
- Profession: Gross receipts > ₹50 lakhs

Form: 3CA/3CB + 3CD
Auditor: Chartered Accountant
Due Date: 30th September (ITR due date)
Report Format: Detailed 50+ clauses covering all aspects

GST Audit:

Annual audit:
- Turnover > ₹5 crores: Mandatory
- Auditor: CA/CMA
- Form: GSTR-9C
- Due: 31st December

Special audit:
- If GST officer suspects tax evasion
- Can order special audit anytime
- Assessee bears cost

Tax Planning Strategies:

1. Choose Right Tax Regime:
- Calculate tax in both old and new regime
- If deductions > ₹2.5 lakhs, old regime usually better
- If salaried with no investments, new regime better

2. Maximize Business Deductions:
- Salaries and wages
- Rent (commercial)
- Interest on business loans
- Professional fees
- Insurance premiums
- Repairs and maintenance
- Telephone and internet
- Travel and conveyance
- Advertisement
- Office supplies
- Depreciation on assets

3. Claim All Eligible Expenses:
- Keep all bills and vouchers
- Ensure expense is:
  * Wholly and exclusively for business
  * Not capital in nature
  * Not personal
  * Properly documented

4. Depreciation Planning:
- Buy assets before year-end
- Claim depreciation (rates: 15-40% depending on asset)
- Additional depreciation: 20% on new plant & machinery

5. Retirement Planning:
- NPS contribution: Deduction under 80CCD
- Pension plans
- PPF contributions

6. Opt for Composition Scheme (if eligible):
- Turnover < ₹1.5 crores
- Pay flat 1% (traders), 5% (restaurants), 6% (others)
- Quarterly return only
- Reduced compliance

7. Time Purchases and Sales:
- Defer income to next year if possible
- Prepay expenses before year-end
- Buy fixed assets in March for full year depreciation

Tax Compliance Calendar:

Monthly:
- 10th: TDS/TCS payment
- 11th: GSTR-1 (sales)
- 20th/22nd/24th: GSTR-3B (summary + payment)
- Last day: Professional tax (state-specific)

Quarterly:

Penalties and Consequences:

Late ITR Filing:
- Fee: ₹5,000 (₹1,000 if income < ₹5 lakhs)
- Interest: 1% per month on unpaid tax
- Loss of refund (if applicable)
- Cannot carry forward losses

Late GST Return:
- GSTR-1: ₹50/day (₹25 CGST + ₹25 SGST), max 0.25% of turnover
- GSTR-3B: Same as above
- Interest: 18% p.a. on unpaid tax

Non-Filing of TDS Return:
- Penalty: ₹200/day, max: tax amount
- Disallowance of expense
- Interest on late payment

Tax Evasion:
- Penalty: 100-300% of tax
- Prosecution possible
- Imprisonment up to 7 years

FAQs:

Q1: Can I file ITR myself or need CA?
A: Simple returns (salary, one business) can be filed yourself on incometax.gov.in. Complex cases benefit from CA help.

Q2: What if I miss advance tax deadline?
A: Interest charged @1% per month on shortfall. Pay ASAP to minimize interest.

Q3: Can I revise return if I made mistake?
A: Yes, within end of relevant assessment year or before completion of assessment (whichever earlier).

Q4: Do I need to file return if income is below taxable limit?
A: Not mandatory, but recommended if:
- Want to claim refund
- Need income proof
- Applying for loans/visa
- Carry forward losses

Q5: Can I claim ITC on vehicle?
A: Generally NO, except for:
- Goods transport vehicles
- Passenger transport as business
- Driving school vehicles
- Dealer/manufacturer

Q6: What is difference between TDS and TCS?
A: TDS = Deducted by payer (buyer of services). TCS = Collected by seller (of goods).

Q7: Can company distribute profits without paying dividend distribution tax?
A: DDT abolished from FY 2020-21. Now dividend taxed in hands of shareholders.

Q8: What is MAT (Minimum Alternate Tax)?
A: Companies must pay minimum 15% tax on book profits if regular tax is lower.

Q9: Can I carry forward business losses?
A: Yes, for 8 years if return filed on time. Cannot carry forward if return filed late.

Q10: What documents to keep for how long?
A: Books of accounts: 6 years from end of relevant financial year. Income tax notices: Keep permanently.
